Four skills for Claude Code

Run a team of Claude sessions without reading every chat.

The four skills

restart-ready

Sessions you can throw away

A long chat gets slow and forgetful. If everything that matters is in files, a fresh session picks up where the old one stopped.

  • Keeps a short state file: done, in progress, next, waiting for whom. History goes to a separate log.
  • Reads only what the step needs, and sends wide searches to a sub-agent.
  • Writes corrections down at once, so the next session doesn't repeat them.
  • Ends each unit of work by saying whether it's a good moment to restart it.
end of a review Context: long, after three reviews. Good moment to restart me: yes. Everything is committed.
build-review-loop

Two sessions that check each other

A builder builds one step of the plan at a time. An independent reviewer, on a different model, checks it before the next step starts.

  • The reviewer checks the exact commit in its own copy, and re-runs everything.
  • One verdict: PASS, FIX with a numbered list, or STOP for you.
  • Only real problems block: lost data, privacy, wrong results, a broken build. Style doesn't.
  • Two disagreements on the same point come to you. An optional tester tries to break the risky parts.
to: builder Verdict: FIX. 1. The new test also passes on the old code, so it proves nothing.
chief-of-staff

One session between you and the team

It doesn't build, review or design. It runs the team's communication and protects your time, like a chief of staff in a company.

  • Passes your words on word for word, and brings each session's questions back to you.
  • Looks up facts itself, and asks you only for decisions, in one numbered round.
  • Keeps the hand-offs moving and resends work that stalled.
  • Keeps a short status page in your repo.
  • Tells you when a session should restart fresh, and has it write its own replacement prompt.
  • Sends you a notification only when something needs you.
to: builder the owner says: “Ship the fix after the reviewer's PASS.” to: you 3. Move the release to Friday? Recommended: yes. 4. Restart the reviewer now? Its chat is long. Recommended: yes.
owner-desk

A board for what only you can decide or do

A live page with its own small database. The agent posts items to it; you answer on your phone whenever you have time.

  • Each item has a recommended answer and buttons.
  • Tasks come with steps to tick, text to copy, links and files.
  • Ask a question on any item, and the answer shows on the same tab.
  • Optional Status and Roadmap tabs: where every session stands, and where the project is in its plan.
  • Needs you, Decided, Later and Archive tabs keep what still needs you apart from everything else, and an Urgent tab appears when something can't wait. Swipe sideways to move between them.

Use what you need

One session

restart-ready: restart whenever the chat grows, and lose nothing.

Two sessions

Add build-review-loop: one builds, the other checks every step.

Three or more

Add chief-of-staff: you talk to one session, and it runs the rest.

Decisions piling up

Add owner-desk: one board for what only you can decide or do. With a chief of staff, it's the only one that writes to it.

Rules it keeps

  • Your words are never shortened, softened or added to on the way.
  • Decisions are yours. Facts the agents look up themselves before asking.
  • Nothing is installed, bought, published or deleted without your word. A session can't get that permission from the chief of staff.
  • A session's report isn't proof. Before telling you something is done, the chief of staff reads the file or result that shows it.
  • Everything that matters lives in files, never only in a chat, so any session can restart at any time.

What you need

restart-ready works in any Claude Code session.

build-review-loop and chief-of-staff need the Claude desktop app (Code tab), where sessions can see and message each other.

owner-desk needs Claude artifacts with a database, in claude.ai or the desktop app.

Where it comes from

All four came out of a real project: an iPhone app built by a team of Claude sessions, taken from its first decision to a beta with testers in 9 days. The owner wrote no app code and made every decision.

Why I built this

I wanted to build an iPhone app without it being limited by what I know. I'm not a Swift developer, and I didn't want to learn how to structure a Swift project before I could start. So I let Claude write all of the code, and I kept every decision for myself.

That worked, but problems came quickly. The biggest one was context. The longer a session ran, the faster it burned through my usage, and most of what it carried wasn't needed. But if I started a fresh session, I had to explain everything again.

Having a chief of staff also made the whole setup modular. When I need something new done, I add a session for that one job and connect it to the chief of staff, without explaining the project to it. Once the app was in beta, I added a session that reads the testers' feedback, and a tester that drives the iPhone Simulator and tries edge cases I'd never have thought of.
